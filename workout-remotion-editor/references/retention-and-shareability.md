# Evidence-informed retention and shareability

Use this layer to make a truthful workout edit easier to start, understand, finish, remember, and share. It extends—not replaces—the workout analysis, editorial integrity, accessibility, and official Remotion routing rules.

## 1. Evidence hierarchy

Apply evidence in this order:

1. the user's explicit goal, audience, platform, brand, and constraints;
2. observable source footage and user-supplied facts;
3. the user's own comparable performance data (retention curve, rewatches, saves, shares, comments, follows, and drop-off points);
4. a controlled variant result;
5. general short-form heuristics.

Never claim an edit will “go viral,” guarantee retention, or describe a heuristic as a platform rule. Platform behavior, trends, and recommendation systems change. When current platform specifications or policies matter, verify them rather than relying on this reference.

## 2. Viewer promise

Write a one-sentence internal viewer promise before selecting the hook: **For [audience], this edit shows [specific value/payoff] through [truthful workout evidence].** Every major beat should set up, advance, clarify, or satisfy that promise. Remove elements that merely delay it.

The opening should work without sound unless preserved audio is essential. In the first meaningful beat:

- begin with readable action, a supported result, a concise question, or a useful tension;
- orient the viewer quickly enough that the movement is not misleading;
- make the subject and value legible without a long logo card;
- preserve the body, load, equipment, and completion evidence needed to judge the moment.

A high-effort rep is only one hook type. Alternatives include a visually clear movement, a technique contrast that the footage truly supports, an instructional promise, a concise workout goal, or an outcome shown with its cause. Do not use pain, apparent failure, body comparison, or dangerous technique as spectacle.

## 3. Honest curiosity and payoff

Curiosity is a gap the edit is prepared to close. State or imply only questions the available footage can answer. Deliver the payoff at a proportionate time; do not repeatedly reset the promise or hide the answer behind irrelevant setup.

Useful short-form structures include:

- **Result → context → progression → payoff:** label the opening as a preview/replay when continuity could be misunderstood.
- **Question → evidence → answer:** suitable for technique or exercise explanation; avoid medical conclusions.
- **Attempt → escalation → completion/reflection:** only when chronology and success are evidenced.
- **Three-part utility:** concise setup, actionable demonstration, memorable summary.

Use pattern changes only when they restore clarity or mark a real beat: a new exercise, angle, scale, pace, overlay, sound event, or visual emphasis. Constant novelty can reduce comprehension. Let important reps breathe.

## 4. Pacing and information density

Treat pacing as information flow rather than cut count. For every segment ask whether it introduces necessary context, advances the workout, supplies evidence, teaches something, or resolves the promise. Trim duplicate setup and dead time before shortening meaningful movement.

Use an internal beat map such as `hook`, `orientation`, `progression`, `payoff`, and optional `close`. Runtime ranges remain soft. A longer complete rep is better than a rapid but misleading fragment. Give instructional text enough time to read and do not stack a new visual, label, caption, and coaching cue at the same instant.

## 5. Shareability without manipulation

Select a primary share reason when supported:

- **utility:** a cue, sequence, or demonstration someone may save or send;
- **identity/community:** a specific, inclusive experience a relevant audience recognizes;
- **achievement/story:** a truthful progression or completion;
- **conversation:** a genuine question with more than one reasonable response;
- **delight/craft:** an impressive or satisfying moment whose context remains intact.

Make useful information self-contained enough to survive sharing. A CTA is optional and should match the value: “Save this sequence” is appropriate only when the sequence is actually reusable; a question should invite substantive discussion rather than demand engagement. Never add fabricated social proof, false urgency, tag-bait, chain-sharing requests, or claims that sharing improves results.

## 6. Endings and loops

Prefer an earned ending: completion, technique takeaway, concise recap, or next-step invitation. A seamless loop is optional. Use it only when the end-to-start transition remains understandable and does not imply an extra rep, continuous set, altered chronology, or a result without its setup. Label replays where ambiguity is possible. Do not cut off the promised payoff merely to force a loop.

## 7. Blueprint fields and scoring

For material retention decisions, record:

- `viewer_promise`, `target_audience`, and `primary_share_reason`;
- beat IDs/types with timeline ranges and their job;
- each hypothesis, supporting evidence, confidence, and risk;
- the payoff location and whether the opening is reordered/replayed;
- optional variant ID and the single major variable changed.

Candidate scoring is a decision aid, not a truth score. Rate supported dimensions such as promise relevance, immediate legibility, movement visibility, technical quality, emotional/story value, useful specificity, continuity, and privacy/rights risk. Do not combine them into false scientific precision. Reject any high-scoring candidate that violates integrity, safety, visibility, or the brief.

## 8. Variants and measurement

When the user requests testing, keep workout truth, core content, export quality, and distribution conditions as comparable as practical. Change one principal hypothesis—such as hook, opening text, or payoff order—per A/B pair. Give variants stable IDs and document the change.

Before publication, define the decision metric and observation window with the user when possible. Diagnose rather than overclaim:

- early drop-off may indicate a weak/unclear opening or an audience mismatch;
- mid-video loss may indicate repetition, delayed payoff, or excess information;
- strong completion with weak saves/shares may indicate entertainment without transferable value;
- rewatches may reflect value **or** confusion, so inspect comments and the actual cut.

Do not compare raw metrics across unlike audiences, durations, platforms, or distribution contexts as if they were controlled. Respect privacy and platform terms when handling analytics.

## 9. Retention QA

Before delivery, verify:

- the first frame/beat is intentional, legible, and honest;
- the viewer promise can be stated and is fulfilled;
- reordered footage, previews, and replays cannot falsify chronology or counts;
- every pattern change has a purpose and important movement stays readable;
- text is comprehensible at playback speed, with safe placement and adequate contrast;
- the primary share reason is supported rather than asserted;
- the ending resolves the edit; any loop preserves workout truth;
- CTAs, claims, comparisons, and analytics statements are supportable;
- accessibility, privacy, rights, and safety are not traded for attention.
