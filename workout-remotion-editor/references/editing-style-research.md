# Editing style research (Phase 10)

Research reviewed 2026-10-04. These sources describe platform or tool behavior;
advertising advice is treated as a transferable editing principle, not as a
promise about organic ranking or workout performance.

## Sources reviewed

- [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes):
  hook/body/close structure, vertical framing, UI-safe space, motion and sensory
  stimulation. The workout transfer is an immediate truthful visual promise,
  readable movement in the body, and a purposeful ending—not a mandatory CTA.
- [TikTok creative advertising guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/what-is-ad-creative-guide):
  hooks within seconds, motion, pace, camera changes, fast cuts, native-feeling
  authenticity, captions, and mobile-first composition. The Director therefore
  uses rapid opening *distributions* but retains complete-rep anchors.
- [YouTube Shorts upload tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12921536):
  Shorts are vertical and may be created without specialist gear. This supports
  a platform bias rather than a speculative ranking formula.
- [YouTube Shorts creation tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948448):
  audio can set tone, but it is optional. Phase 10 records future rhythm cut
  opportunities and keeps the project's muted-source default.
- [Adobe Premiere Pro time remapping](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-pro/using/duration-speed.html):
  keyframed speed changes and eased transitions support gradual speed ramps.
  Phase 10 describes renderer-neutral cubic-Bézier-like keyframes and does not
  claim optical flow exists.
- [Remotion transitions](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitioning):
  `TransitionSeries` separates presentation from linear/spring timing, overlaps
  scene duration during transitions, supports overlays, and imposes adjacency
  and duration constraints. The edit plan chooses semantics; React implements.
- [Remotion timing and trimming](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/timing):
  timeline placement, trimming, duration, and playback rate have a defined order.
  Source-to-composition mapping must be recomputed after any accepted speed map.

## Conclusions applied

1. A hook is a truthful opening promise, not license to invent a repetition.
2. Energy comes primarily from timing, motion, and contrast. An effect on every
   cut reduces clarity; hard cuts remain dominant in energetic profiles.
3. Short-form rhythm needs variable micro/short/medium clips plus periodic
   complete anchors. A single fixed duration produces mechanical pacing.
4. Cinematic/trailer feeling comes from mixed lengths, setup/detail contrast,
   deliberate pauses, escalation, and a hero ending more than flashy effects.
5. Setup and recovery images can be useful connective breathers, but should not
   dominate or replace reviewed movement.
6. Speed ramps need eased keyframes around a reviewed landmark. Strong slowdown
   is conservative below 50 fps when no frame interpolation pipeline exists.
7. Beat sensitivity is an opportunity layer. Workout chronology and complete
   movement remain higher priority than hitting every beat.
